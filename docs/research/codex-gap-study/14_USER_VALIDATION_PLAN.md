# User validation and superiority proof plan

Research date: **2026-10-05**. Repository: `rebuild/livelift-next`. This is research and a proposed product strategy, not implementation or proof of market demand. The [V3 master roadmap](../../roadmap/LIVELIFT_V3_MASTER_ROADMAP.md) governs future work; these reports supply evidence.

## Goal and sample

Test whether the manual workflow saves operational work compared with **TikTok LIVE Manager + a good spreadsheet/chat setup**, and whether a configured professional rundown is already sufficient. Recruit **5–10 relevant participants** across at least three recurring teams: operators/team leads plus hosts for the cue test. Aim for at least three dedicated operators and three hosts; report roles and denominator separately. Realistic proxies are acceptable only when clearly identified and experienced enough to run the tasks; they cannot establish merchant willingness to pay.

Target VN merchant teams/small agencies running repeated shows with multiple products and agreed promotion times. Include a competent spreadsheet user and a skeptical operator. Exclude only-analytics/solo workflows from the primary claim; retain one simple-show counterexample to expose where LiveLift is unnecessary. No interviews or recruiting messages have been sent in this task.

## Baseline fairness

Use the participant's actual native account where permitted, with its real Manager/Console/scripts availability. Ask them to show product ordering, suggestions, rehearsal, diagnosis and any native timing/plan controls before asserting a gap. Prepare a competent sheet with planned times, duration formulas, required products, anchor highlights and an actual-notes column. Retain their normal Zalo/chat or a consented message counter. Do not intentionally handicap the baseline.

At least two operators additionally try a commerce template in Ontime, Shoflo or Rundown Studio, subject to available access. Test Opsique hands-on if an accessible trial exists; otherwise record that direct competitor uncertainty. No purchases or contact messages are authorized by this report.

## Paired protocol

1. Obtain consent for task observation and screen/event recording. Avoid customer/order/comment personal data. Measure their baseline setup and a short familiar task first.
2. Give matched training in both tools, then a 12–15 minute compressed show scenario with fixed seed/time anchors, several products, optional Q&A and closing commitment. Prototype/Wizard-of-Oz assistance must be disclosed and scored separately from functional software.
3. Randomly or counterbalance **A→B / B→A** order across operators. Use equivalent but distinct scenario content to reduce memorization. Preserve the same difficulty, promotion urgency and number of disturbances.
4. Introduce a pitch overrun, late promotion report, skipped required product and a handoff/reconnect event in predefined positions. Observe recovery choices; do not steer toward LiveLift's recommended choice.
5. Ask participants to identify timing/coverage facts, then prepare tomorrow's plan using selected changes. Verify the resulting plan actually changed and remains feasible.
6. Test optional Host View separately with matched cue trials and native script/chat baseline. Separate coordination benefit from operator desk benefit.
7. Conduct a brief workload/preference interview, ask what they would keep/remove and test a concrete repeat-use/purchase scenario. Record negative cases and reasons.

If only a fixture prototype exists, first run a disclosed concept comparison with manual computation/Wizard-of-Oz; passing it authorizes a functional P0 build, not a claim of measured backend reliability. Repeat the core comparison on the functional checkpoint before external superiority claims.

## Preregistered decision thresholds

These are **proposed pass/fail targets**, not observed results. Freeze scenario scripts, timer definitions and exclusion rules before first scored task. Report raw counts and medians with the tiny sample; do not imply statistical significance.

| Measure | Operational definition | Initial pass threshold | Fail / response |
|---|---|---|---|
| Setup time | From blank/template choice to ready coherent show | ≤10m and no more than 2m slower than baseline median | Simplify import/planning or abandon heavy setup. |
| Timing awareness | Time from injected forecast conflict to participant correctly stating at-risk anchor | ≥80% of scored trials within 10s; report exact n | If native/sheet equally clear, differentiation narrows. |
| Recovery time | Conflict injection → confirmed feasible plan/cue recovery | Median ≥30% faster than baseline; ≥90% choices respect constraints | No improvement after two focused iterations: pivot/template/stop. |
| Cue misses | Required time-critical cue absent or beyond predefined tolerance (e.g. 15s in rehearsal) | No increase vs baseline; zero critical unintended misses | Reliability blocks release regardless of preference. |
| Capture burden | Extra effort to record routine transition | ≤1 routine command/transition and median ≤3s; ≥90% boundaries within 15s of observed truth | Reduce logging; review cannot be trusted if capture fails. |
| Operator workload | Same short workload scale or NASA-TLX for both tools | ≥20% lower median, or no worse with clear recovery benefit | Added screen cost may erase advantage. |
| Host coordination | Cue comprehension and avoidable timing messages | ≥80% cues correctly understood within 5s; ≥30% fewer timing/next-cue messages | Remove/defer Host View if it fails or harms delivery. |
| Review + next plan | Correct variance findings plus feasible changed rundown | ≤5m total and ≥30% faster median; ≥90% facts correct | Review/adaptation is not a retention layer yet. |
| Subjective preference | Voluntary choice for another relevant show after both tasks | ≥70% operators choose LiveLift, with specific reason | Preferences alone cannot override missed-cue risk. |
| Actual repeat use | Subsequent recurring shows, not a promised intention | At least 3 teams voluntarily use across ≥3 sessions and apply a useful selected change | Reassess wedge if novelty wears off. |

Host View requires its own gate: message reduction **and** comprehension, with no observed worsening of product delivery judged by host/operator. If it fails, desk P0 may still pass.

## Data collection and analysis

Record participant role, experience, scenario/order, tool/version, actual native features, whether tasks were assisted, injected event time, recognition/command/cue time, correctness, message count category, review facts, next-plan validity, workload and preference reason. Retain a minimal consented observation sheet and anonymous IDs. A message count alone can be gamed by silence; combine it with cue correctness and host quality.

Compute per-operator paired differences and sample medians, include every negative case and disclose missed/unrecorded observations. A formative sample is not sufficient for population-wide superiority, revenue effects or causal GMV claims. Increase sample only if a meaningful workflow signal appears.

## Field checkpoint and willingness to pay

After a functional P0 passes, run a repeat-show pilot with at least three teams. Check whether setup becomes faster, actual capture remains accurate, patches are reused and the team voluntarily returns. Explore a concrete price/savings tradeoff through interviews or owner-led offers; no price is recommended as proven. Production billing is not required for an internal validation checkpoint.

## Pivot and kill

If two focused iterations cannot improve recovery/review over a fair sheet baseline, stop expanding the desk. If a configured rundown already wins, test a commerce template or review companion. If users want only native metrics/scripts, reject the wedge. If actual capture repeatedly fails, stop learning claims. If Host View fails, remove it. If API access fails, keep the proven manual product and drop enrichment promises.
