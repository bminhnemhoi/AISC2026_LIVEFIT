### Direct threat: Opsique

Opsique advertises the same broad commerce preparation → host/operator execution → review loop under consideration. Its host/studio outputs and recurring team operations narrow LiveLift's novelty claim substantially. Advertised automatic TikTok controls must not be taken as official API support; advertised cost/performance improvements lack an independent evaluation in this study. The correct response is a side-by-side workflow trial, not dismissal because its implementation or customer base is unknown. If an accessible Opsique plan already gives the target teams the same benefit at lower total cost, a new broad desk should be killed or narrowed.

### Stronger timing substitutes than an analytics comparison suggests

Shoflo's changelog, not just older help pages, matters: multiple fixed anchors are now documented. Ontime and Rundown Studio likewise make a basic “schedule shifts when a segment overruns” demo insufficient novelty. LiveLift must beat a configured commerce template on action-capture burden, promotion constraint clarity and next-show adaptation. A timer with product names is not a defensible product.

### StreamLine AI identity and overlap

The supplied name was resolved to the official site and a publisher extension listing, avoiding unrelated legal-services products with similar names. The observed publisher claims include native-dashboard reading and pitch-context coaching. We did not verify claimed GMV multipliers, DOM access reliability or current users. It competes with AI pitch assistance and historical coaching; LiveLift should not use those as an uncontested wedge.

## Substitute map: the strongest baseline is already in the studio

| Substitute | What users already get | Failure to observe before replacing | LiveLift must prove |
|---|---|---|---|
| TikTok LIVE Manager alone | Native commerce actions, activities, scripts and analysis | Operator can keep a simple show mentally; a new screen may distract | A genuinely missed timing/coordination job in that account. |
| TikTok Manager + Google Sheets | Product list, duration formulas, planned times, checkboxes, shared editing/history | Poorly configured sheets are an unfair baseline; Apps Script/manual logs can cover much of our model | Faster recovery and review with less ongoing maintenance. |
| Zalo + spreadsheet | Familiar host/operator messaging plus cheap shared plan | Messaging may be adequate, especially for short/simple shows | Reduce avoidable cue messages without hiding urgent communication. |
| Notion | Templates, product notes, shared session history | More flexible notes may be sufficient; formulas can cover static planning | Live timing/state consistency and reuse need enough value to justify switching. |
| Discord / Slack | Channels, role handoff, pinned instructions, searchable decisions | Existing team organization may outweigh a bespoke desk | Private cue projection and fewer coordination steps, not another full chat tool. |
| Paper rundown | Fast glanceable plan, no battery/network setup | Operator can annotate actuals cheaply for a small show | Dynamic recovery and repeated planning must be frequent enough to matter. |
| OBS notes / studio monitor | Script and next-product reminder adjacent to broadcast controls | Another display might increase cognitive load | Better cue recognition, not more text. |
| Shoflo / Ontime / Rundown Studio + commerce template | Proven timing/cue output, reusable rundown | Commodity fields and templates may already solve the majority of the job | Meaningful commerce-specific recovery/operational-learning savings. |

These substitute abilities are workflow analysis, not a claim of authenticated trials. “Sheets cannot do it” is false: a sufficiently configured spreadsheet and discipline can express plans, actuals and comparisons. The possible advantage is lower recurring operating effort and fewer state errors.

## Professional rundown vocabulary and transfer

| Concept | Professional meaning | Commerce transfer | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Planned duration | Allocated segment time | Product pitch, Q&A, promotion announcement, closing | P0 |
| Actual duration | Elapsed/completed execution | What operator recorded happened, not what was scheduled | P0 |
| Over / under | Actual or projected deviation | Recovery awareness; must distinguish current elapsed from final variance | P0 |
| Projected start | Forecast from prior execution/remaining durations | Show which product or promotion is becoming late | P0 |
| Fixed start / hard anchor | Must-start wall-clock constraint | Agreed sale/giveaway announcement or closing deadline; human may still miss it | P0 |
| Floating segment | Follows previous execution | Most product pitches and Q&A | P0 |
| Downstream cascade | Changes propagate through later timing | Forecast only; never silently rewrite agreed plan | P0 |
| Hold | Delay a cue or pause a production timer | Keep current product pending decision; live wall clock and promotion deadline never pause | Modify semantics |
| Skip | Remove pending item from execution | Record skipped product / optional Q&A; required coverage warning | P0 |
| Extend / shorten | Change active target / allocation | Operator recovery with minimum selling time; preserve baseline | P0 |
| Reorder | Change pending sequence | Only eligible pending items; do not move executed history | P0 |
| Standby | Prepare next element | One short upcoming cue; avoid separate status ceremony for every product | P0.5 if needed |
| Take / go | Authorized start of element | Start next segment, separate from a native promotion execution report | P0 |
| Presenter / operator view | Different role-specific information | Host gets glanceable selling/timing cue; operator gets recovery context | VALIDATE P0.5 |
| Proof of performance | Record delivery evidence | Timestamped operator report vs independently observed native action | Minimal P0 report; enrichment later |


The timing concepts are established by the professional documentation above. LiveLift's design contribution can be commerce constraints and the closed adaptation workflow, not invention of countdown, GO, cascading time or presenter view.

## Fair “why not just use…” answers

| Alternative | Credible conditional answer | When the answer fails |
|---|---|---|
| TikTok LIVE Manager | Keep it for platform actions. Add LiveLift only when timed future commitments and operational adaptation are hard to manage natively. | Native account workflow handles those commitments, or the team's show is simple. |
| Sheets | LiveLift can avoid manual formula repair and repeated reconstruction, with recorded transitions and constraint-aware forecasts. | A good template performs just as well at lower effort/cost. |
| Shoflo | Commerce snapshots, promotion/coverage constraints and approved next-show patches may reduce setup/review work. | Custom columns/template plus existing rundown already meets the need. |
| FastMoss | It answers market/performance questions; LiveLift would own a team's agreed operational plan and execution history. | Customer primarily wants product discovery, creator intelligence or market GMV. |
| Kalodata | Same distinction; do not deny its documented live monitoring and script capabilities. | Analytics/coaching is the actual job, not timing recovery. |
| Zalo + spreadsheet | Shared timed cues can replace repetitive “next/stop/late” messages while retaining normal chat. | Host comprehension or production quality worsens, or messages were not a real burden. |

These answers remain hypotheses until report 14's paired tests. If a configured substitute wins, prefer integration/template specialization over a broad rebuild.
